LAYERS @ Stony Brook

Earth history, one layer at a time.

340 Earth & Space Science Bldg

Stony Brook University

Stony Brook, NY 11794

The LAYERS group (Landscape Archives and Earth Systems) reconstructs how water, ice, and fire have reshaped landscapes over the last several hundred thousand years, and how abruptly those changes occurred.

We read the geologic record like a photo album, watching time and space shift from one layer to the next. On foot, across the water, or from the air, we map, log, and correlate sediments and landforms, then stitch those snapshots into a time framework with high-precision geochronology. We work in the Great Basin, the Grand Canyon region, on Long Island, and in northwest Washington.

What we measure. We work in Stony Brook’s isotope geochemistry facility (FIRST Lab), which houses a Nu Plasma 3 multi-collector ICP-MS, an Agilent 8900 triple-quadrupole ICP-MS, and two IsotopX Phoenix thermal ionization mass spectrometers. We measure boron isotopes (δ11B) in waters, carbonates, and plant material, trace elements and rare earth elements in natural waters, sediments, and carbonates, and U-Pb ages in carbonates and accessory minerals. We are establishing in-house U-series dating, with routine analyses expected in 2027, building on our published U-Th chronologies from the western United States.

Current directions. Abrupt climate change · hydroclimate · glacier and ice-sheet chronologies · boron isotopes · wildfires (in development) · winds (in development). See Projects.

We welcome creative ideas, unconventional directions, and collaborations that challenge standard approaches and wisdom.

Joining the group. Please reach out by email if you are undergraduate, a prospective graduate student, or interested in postdoctoral work. I am happy to develop fellowship applications with prospective students and postdocs. A short note on what you are curious about is the best place to start.
