The LAYERS group (Landscape Archives and Earth Systems) reconstructs how water, ice, and fire reshape landscapes through geologic time, and how abruptly those shifts occur. We read the geologic record by mapping, logging, and dating sediments and landforms.

#### Active research questions

- **[Hydroclimate dynamics](/projects/1_project/):** How far and how fast did rain belts shift across the western United States during the last glaciation?
- **Ice-sheet chronologies:** When did the [Laurentide](/projects/2_project/) and [Cordilleran](/projects/3_project/) ice sheets reach their maximum limits, and why do dating methods disagree? (Funded by WA State & USGS)
- **[Isotope geochemistry of wildfire](/projects/4_project/) (in development):** Can fire leave an isotopic fingerprint that survives in waters and sediments?

#### Facility and analytical capabilities

The LAYERS group works in Stony Brook's Facility for Isotope Research and Student Training (FIRST), co-led by Troy Rasbury and Guleed Ali. Its strengths are boron isotopes and U-Pb dating of carbonates, supported by multi-collector and triple-quadrupole ICP-MS, TIMS, and class 100 and class 1000 clean labs.

**Recent.** Carbonate U/Th ages confirm the Mono Basin excursion as the Laschamps geomagnetic excursion ([GSA Bulletin, 2026](https://doi.org/10.1130/B38358.1)).
